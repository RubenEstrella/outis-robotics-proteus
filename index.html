02 · The problem

The first look inside is still taken by a person.

Process vessels, collapsed buildings, damaged hulls: someone climbs in to find out what is there and to do the work. The robots that exist each fit one part of the job. The ones that walk don't fit through the opening; the ones that fit can't push.

Send a robot. Lose a module, not a person.

03 · The shift

Every fixed-shape robot fails one phase of the job.

Today a robot is built for the hardest moment of its task and keeps that shape for the whole mission. Proteus takes the strongest shape for every phase: it walks, passes the opening as a chain, braces on site and works with force, then walks back. 04 · Why it works

Enter as a chain. Work as a structure.

In a chain, actuators about 2 cm from each joint axis hold the tool load, so force and stiffness collapse with reach. Braced, two supports about 40 cm apart hold it as a force couple and the struts carry load along their axes. Force grows with that lever ratio, stiffness with its square.

Tool force
35× 60.7 N braced vs. 1.7 N as a chain, same four MK1.1 modules, tool 1.0 m deep
Stiffness
560× 15.7 N/mm braced; 15× and 117× even against two parallel chains
Limit we report
1.1× out of the plane of both supports; a second wall foot makes a spatial truss

05 · The module

Everything is made of one part.

An actuated strut with a docking head at each end. Built many times over, it becomes whatever the job needs. That part, its docks and its reconfiguration under load are what we build, protect and qualify.

  1. Docking headA truncated octahedron with seven load-bearing dock faces, 14 per module, mating in three orientations; hooks interlock with the partner head.
  2. Six degrees of freedomAt each end, three self-locking linear actuators tilt the head by up to 50° and extend it: a head-to-head span of 410 to 610 mm.
  3. One strut, many rolesLeg, beam, chain link or brace: the same part, about 110 mm across and 0.45 m long.
  4. Power through the dockSpring contacts pass 24 V between modules. Tool and sensor modules carry the same head: a gripper with force sensing and camera, an ultrasonic or camera probe, brace feet.

Utility model filed

German utility model application with 34 claims on the robot module, its coupling head and the modular robot system, filed with the DPMA on 21 Oct 2026 by both founders. Interface specifications will be published so others can build tools and hosts for Proteus; module internals stay proprietary.

06 · First job

We start where the access is defined.

Retrieving and placing objects without human entry. Stage 1 proves it in a guarded mock plant hall: today such work means opening the equipment or a confined-space entry with permits and a standby attendant.

First

Energy and industrial plants

Vessels, columns and exchangers: retrieve, inspect and place sensors through nozzles and manways. No confined-space entry, fewer openings, less downtime per intervention.

Next

Civil protection and rescue

Check collapsed structures and voids before anyone enters. The first look costs a module, not a rescuer.

Dual-use

Defense maintenance and protection

Aircraft fuel tanks, vehicle hulls, ordnance disposal and CBRN reconnaissance. Never weapons, munitions or fire control.

07 · Plan

From one module to the full mission.

The final demonstration: an independent lab runs the whole mission on a course it sets up. Magazine, quadruped, chain through the opening, manipulator on site, measurement, return. Every stage adds one part of it.

  1. Oct 2026 · built

    MK1 module, powered head motion, load-bearing docks, docking test rig, utility model.

  2. Stage 1 · to Sep 2027

    From a magazine through a Ø200 mm opening as a chain, braced manipulator, measurement at 1.0 m, withdrawal. Against a snake control and the strongest commercial arm.

  3. Stage 2 · to Jul 2028

    Chain travel to the work site, mock plant and collapse sections, 5,000-cycle aging, integration on an external robot.

  4. Stage 3 · to Mar 2029

    The quadruped walks to the opening with double-force MK3 drives; 100 N and 10 N/mm at 1.0 m.

  5. Stage 4 · to Dec 2029

    The full mission by an independent lab, at least 18 of 20 complete; paid pilots; series design.

Gates in Stage 1 G0 month 2.5: test course and protocol frozen, dock pair measured · G1 month 5: the chain closes into the manipulator, at least 10× the snake's force predicted from measured docks · G2 month 10.5: targets measured, subset repeated by an outside lab.
