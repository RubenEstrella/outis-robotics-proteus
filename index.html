01 · The problem

Someone still has to go in first.

Process vessels, columns, voids in collapsed buildings: today the work behind a small opening means opening the equipment or a confined-space entry with permits and a standby attendant. Tools slim enough to fit cannot apply force at reach; robots strong enough to work do not fit.

Send a robot. Lose a module, not a person.

02 · The shift

Every fixed-shape robot fails one phase of the job.

A fixed robot is built for the hardest moment of its mission and carries that shape everywhere. Proteus changes shape per phase: it walks, slips through as a chain, braces and works with force, then walks back.

04 · The module

Everything is made of one part.

An actuated strut with a docking head at each end. Built many times over, it becomes whatever the job needs. That part, its docks and its reconfiguration under load are what we build, protect and qualify.

  1. Docking headA dedicated interface connects modules into load-bearing structures and supports reconfiguration for different tasks.
  2. Six degrees of freedomAt each end, three self-locking linear actuators tilt the head by up to 50° and extend it: a head-to-head span of 410 to 610 mm.
  3. One strut, many rolesLeg, beam, chain link or brace: the same part, about 110 mm across and 0.45 m long.
  4. Power through the dockThe connection supports power sharing within the structure. Compatible tools and sensors extend what the module system can do.

05 · Why it works

Enter as a chain. Work as a structure.

Proteus changes its load-bearing geometry after access. The modules enter as a slender chain, then form a locally braced working structure with parallel load paths. The actuated struts carry load along their axes while the structure moves its tool.

Access, reconfigure, work

The working structure is built at the task.

Local bracing and parallel struts create the load paths needed for the tool force. Supports and working configurations are selected for the task and qualified together.

The comparison is with the compact Reach Alpha 5 manipulator. It concerns working capability after access, with Proteus bracing declared explicitly.

Force target ratio
10.2× nominal target/reference ratio: 200 N divided by the Reach Alpha 5 published 2 kg lift-load equivalent (19.6 N). A development target, not a measured improvement.
Tool force target
200 N Stage 1: held for 60 s with at most 5 mm tool displacement at 18 of 20 positions, across at least 3 braced configurations. Physical qualification pending.
Configuration target
200+ targeting more than 200 physically validated configurations across 3 structure classes: serial access, locally braced work and legged locomotion, with at least 50 per class. Overall development target; Stage 1 interim goal: at least 4 configurations across 2 classes. Physical validation pending.

The experimental force ratio will be measured at common reachable positions with matched task direction, hold time and displacement limit. Mounting, supports and operating conditions will be reported.

06 · First job

We start where the access is defined.

Retrieving and placing objects without human entry in hazardous, confined or unstructured spaces. Stage 1 tests it in a guarded mock plant hall with three equipment mock-ups; Stage 2 moves the task family onto real equipment with a plant partner.

First

Energy and industrial plants

Vessels, columns and exchangers: retrieve, inspect and place sensors through nozzles and manways. No confined-space entry, fewer openings, less downtime per intervention.

Next

Civil protection and rescue

Inspect collapsed structures and voids before anyone enters. The first look costs a module, not a rescuer.

Dual-use

Defense maintenance and protection

Aircraft fuel tanks, vehicle hulls, ordnance disposal and CBRN reconnaissance. Never weapons, munitions or fire control.

07 · Mission and vision

Robots that bring their own structure.

Built in Europe, one module family becomes the structure each job needs. Our goal: keep people out of hazardous spaces and put the same fleet to work on the next task.
