01 · The problem

Someone still has to go in first.

Process vessels, columns, voids in collapsed buildings: today the work behind a small opening means opening the equipment or a confined-space entry with permits and a standby attendant. Tools slim enough to fit cannot apply force at reach; robots strong enough to work do not fit.

Send a robot. Lose a module, not a person.

05 · Execution

Prove each step.
Then scale.

The prototype is the starting point. Our plan links hardware qualification, customer validation and integration before the full mission.

  1. Stage 1 / Qualification

    Make the working structure reliable.

    Test access, reconfiguration and withdrawal on the rig. Qualify the 200 N working-force target across braced configurations.

    Evidence: repeatable hardware tests, measured operating limits and an independent repeat of a test subset.

  2. Stage 2 / Integration

    Move beyond the rig.

    Extend testing to representative plant environments, aging cycles and integration with an external robot.

    Evidence: durability results and successful integration on a host system.

  3. Stages 3 and 4 / Full mission

    Connect the complete task.

    Add the walking approach and return, repeat the end-to-end mission independently and prepare for series design.

    Evidence: complete mission runs, retained performance after aging and paid pilot work.

Hiring and module builds follow measured milestones. The compact MK4 line develops alongside this qualification path; further miniaturization remains the longer-term ambition.

07 · The module

Everything is made of one part.

One module, combined into many structures. We are building and qualifying the foundation today, with smaller generations planned for finer structures and narrower access.

  1. Docking headA dedicated interface connects modules into load-bearing structures and supports reconfiguration for different tasks.
  2. Six degrees of freedomAt each end, three self-locking linear actuators tilt the head by up to 50° and extend it: a head-to-head span of 410 to 610 mm.
  3. One strut, many rolesLeg, beam, chain link or brace: the same part, about 110 mm across and 0.45 m long.
  4. Power through the dockThe connection supports power sharing within the structure. Compatible tools and sensors extend what the module system can do.
